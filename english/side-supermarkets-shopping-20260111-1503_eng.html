🛒Cheap Supermarkets & Shopping

The "Big Three" Discount Supermarkets

For the absolute lowest prices on staples (water, dairy, snacks, and toiletries), look for these three chains. They are the Turkish equivalent of Aldi or Lidl: small, no-frills, and highly affordable.

Supermarket Best For... Pro Tip

BİM Lowest overall prices on basic staples. Look for their "Dost" dairy brand; the yogurt and milk are excellent quality for the price.

A101 Weekly "Aktüel" deals on household goods. They often stock surprisingly good electronics or beach gear on Thursdays at deep discounts.

Şok Fresh produce and daily essentials. Their "Mis" brand dairy is a local favorite. They often have better fruit/veg sections than BİM.

Note: These discount stores do not sell alcohol or tobacco. If you need these items, you will need to visit a Migros or a local "Tekel" shop.

Mid-Range & Full-Service Supermarkets

If you want a wider selection of international brands, a fresh butcher counter, or a cold beer, head to these larger stores.

Migros: The most famous chain in Turkey. They use a "M" ranking system: a Migros Jet is a small corner shop, while a 5M Migros is a massive hypermarket. The Migros near the Side Saturday Market area is a great one-stop shop.

CarrefourSA: Similar to Migros, offering a mix of local and imported goods. Prices are slightly higher than BİM but the selection is much better.

The Local Bazaars (Pazars)

For the freshest and cheapest fruits, vegetables, olives, and spices, you must visit the weekly markets. Buying produce here is significantly cheaper than in any supermarket.

Saturday: Side Neighborhood Market

Located near the Side Mosque (Side Camii), this market is the most convenient for those staying in the resort center.

Hours: 08:00 – 13:00 (approx).

Budget Tip: Go after 12:00 PM; vendors often drop prices to clear stock before closing.

Monday & Thursday: Manavgat Grand Bazaar

Manavgat is only 10 minutes from Side by dolmuş. This is the largest market in the region.

Monday: Mostly food, produce, and textiles.

Thursday: Heavy focus on clothing and "genuine fake" designer goods, but still has a massive food section.

Shopping Strategy: How To Save More

The "Tourist Tax" in Old Town: Avoid buying bottled water or snacks in the "Ancient Side" harbor area. A bottle of water there can cost 5x more than at a BİM just 500 meters away.

The Migros Money Card: If you’re staying for more than a few days, ask the cashier at Migros for a "Money Card." It costs a few Lira but gives you instant access to "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" deals and member-only discounts.

Lira vs. Euro: While many shops in Side accept Euro or GBP, the exchange rate used by small shops is usually poor. Always pay in Turkish Lira (TRY) at supermarkets to ensure you get the official bank rate.

Bring Your Own Bags: Like much of Europe, Turkish supermarkets charge for plastic bags. Bring a reusable tote to save a few Kuruş and help the environment.

Essential Turkish Grocery Vocabulary

İndirim: Discount / Sale

Ekmek: Bread (Standard loaves are very cheap and regulated by the government)

Manav: Greengrocer/Produce section

For those staying in the Kumköy or Evrenseki areas, look for the local "Migros Jet" or "A101" locations situated along the main boulevard; these are strategically placed to serve the holiday apartments at local prices.
