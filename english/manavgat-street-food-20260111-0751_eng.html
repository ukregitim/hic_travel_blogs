Manavgat

🥙Street Food Guide

Top Street Foods You Must Try

Originally from Mersin but perfected across Southern Turkey, Tantuni is a local favorite. It consists of julienned beef or lamb stir-fried in a traditional circular metal pan (a sac). The meat is seasoned with sumac, parsley, and tomatoes, then wrapped in a thin, pillowy flatbread called lavaş.

The Pro Tip: Ask for "Acılı" (spicy) to get a hit of local pepper flakes that cut through the richness of the meat.

Gözleme (Anatolian Flatbread)

Often referred to as the "Turkish Crepe," Gözleme is hand-rolled dough folded over various fillings and cooked on a dome-shaped griddle. In Manavgat, you’ll find women at the local markets rolling these out with incredible speed.

Popular Fillings: Peynirli (feta and parsley), Patatesli (spiced potato), or Ispanaklı (spinach).

Best Paired With: A cold glass of frothy Ayran (salted yogurt drink).

Midye Dolma (Stuffed Mussels)

As the sun sets, you will see vendors appearing with large trays of Midye Dolma. These are orange-hued mussels stuffed with herbed rice, currants, and pine nuts.

How to Eat: The vendor will squeeze fresh lemon juice over the mussel for you. Use the top shell as a spoon to scoop out the rice and meat in one bite. It is addictive and surprisingly affordable.

The quintessential Turkish breakfast on the go. Simit is a circular bread encrusted with sesame seeds and molasses. In Manavgat, the crust is particularly crunchy.

Where to find it: Look for the red glass carts scattered throughout the city center. It’s best enjoyed fresh in the morning before the sun softens the crunch.

For the adventurous eater, Kokoreç is a staple of Turkish "after-dark" food culture. It is made of spiced lamb intestines wrapped around seasoned offal, grilled horizontally over a charcoal fire, then finely chopped with tomatoes and green peppers.

The Flavor Profile: It is smoky, fatty, and deeply savory, served inside half a loaf of crusty bread.

The Sweet Finish: Street Desserts

Known as the "Brothel Dessert" historically (though now a family-friendly staple), this is a circular, deep-fried dough soaked in thick sugar syrup. It is crunchy on the outside and syrupy on the inside.

Taze Mısır (Fresh Corn)

Depending on the season, you will find vendors selling corn either Közde (grilled over charcoal for a smoky char) or Süt Mısır (boiled in a pot with salt and butter).

Essential Local Knowledge

The Monday Market: Manavgat hosts one of the largest open-air markets in the region every Monday and Thursday. This is the absolute "ground zero" for authentic Gözleme and fresh seasonal fruits like figs and pomegranate juice.

Regional Citrus: Manavgat is famous for its citrus groves. Do not pass a street vendor with a manual press without ordering a glass of Nar Suyu (Pomegranate juice) or Portakal Suyu (Orange juice)—it is some of the freshest in the world.

Service Hours: While Simit vendors are early birds, Tantuni and Kokoreç stalls typically peak in the late afternoon and remain open well past midnight to cater to the local nightlife.
