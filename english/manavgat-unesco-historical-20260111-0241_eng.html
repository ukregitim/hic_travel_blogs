Manavgat

🏛️UNESCO & Historical Places

Ancient City Of Aspendos (Unesco Tentative List)

Distance from Manavgat: ~35 km (30 mins west)

The Highlight: The Roman Theater Aspendos is home to the best-preserved Roman theater in the world. Built in the 2nd century AD during the reign of Marcus Aurelius, it is so intact that it is still used for opera and ballet festivals today.

The Theater: Climb to the top row to test the legendary acoustics; a whisper on stage can be heard perfectly at the top.

The Aqueducts: Just behind the city, you can view the massive Roman siphon aqueducts, an engineering marvel that once supplied the city with water.

Alanya Castle & Dockyard (Unesco Tentative List)

Distance from Manavgat: ~60 km (1 hour east)

The Highlight: Seljuk Naval Architecture Perched on a rocky peninsula jutting into the sea, Alanya (historically Coracesium) was a stronghold for Cilician pirates and later the Seljuk Turks.

The Citadel (Kale): A 6km defensive wall with 140 towers.

The Red Tower (Kızılkule): An iconic octagonal tower protecting the harbor.

The Seljuk Shipyard (Tersane): The only remaining Seljuk dockyard in Turkey, featuring five vaulted archways where warships were built.

Archaeological Site Of Perge (Unesco Tentative List)

Distance from Manavgat: ~55 km (45 mins west)

The Highlight: The Colonnaded Street Perge was one of the wealthiest cities of the ancient world and the capital of Pamphylia. It is famous for its distinct architecture and connection to St. Paul the Apostle.

The Stadium: One of the largest intact ancient stadiums, which could hold 12,000 spectators.

The Water Channel: A unique water channel runs down the center of the main colonnaded street, designed to cool the city during hot summers.

II. The Crown Jewel: Ancient Side

Located immediately adjacent to Manavgat (practically the same city), Side is an open-air museum where modern life exists inside ancient ruins.

The Ancient City of Side

Side was a major port city and a center for the slave trade in antiquity. Today, the ruins are uniquely intertwined with souvenir shops and cafes.

The Temple of Apollo: The symbol of Side. These white marble columns standing against the deep blue Mediterranean are the most photographed spot in the region. Tip: Go at sunset for the "golden hour" view.

The Roman Theater: Unlike other theaters built into hillsides, Side's theater was built on flat ground using massive stone arches—a prime example of Roman architectural confidence.

The Vespasian Gate: The monumental entrance to the old city.

III. Hidden Historical Treasures (Off the Beaten Path)

If you want to escape the tour buses, these sites offer history in solitude.

Seleukeia (Lyrbe)

Location: ~15 km north of Manavgat (in the mountains)

Vibe: Indiana Jones exploration. Hidden deep within a pine forest, this site is often empty. The city was built on a hilltop for defense against pirates. The Agora (market square) is remarkably well-preserved, surrounded by two-story forest-covered ruins.

Access: Requires a car; the road is winding and scenic.

Selge & The Adam Rocks

Location: ~60-80 km north (high in the Taurus Mountains, near Köprülü Canyon)

Vibe: Dramatic and rugged. Selge was a Pisidian city that fiercely maintained its independence. The ruins of its theater sit at a dizzying altitude with a backdrop of jagged mountain peaks. Nearby, you can see the "Adam Rocks" (Conglomerate Rocks), which look like standing human figures formed by nature.

Alara Han & Castle

Location: ~35 km east of Manavgat

Vibe: Silk Road history. Built by the Seljuks in 1231, Alara Han is a Caravanserai (a roadside inn for travelers). It was a vital stop on the Silk Road. Above it, on a steep conical hill, sits the Alara Castle.

Warning: The climb to the castle is steep and involves tunnels; bring a flashlight and sturdy shoes.

IV. Practical Travel Tips

Best Time to Visit April-May or Sept-Oct. Mid-summer (July/Aug) can reach 40°C+, making ruin-walking difficult.

Dolmuş (Minibuses): Frequent cheap rides run from Manavgat center to Side and Alanya.

Car Rental: Highly recommended for visiting Aspendos, Perge, and Seleukeia efficiently.

Museum Pass If visiting Perge, Aspendos, and Side Theater, buy the Museum Pass The Mediterranean (Mobile App available) to save money and skip lines.

What to Wear Sturdy walking shoes are mandatory. The marble streets in Side and Perge are uneven and slippery.

A Final Pro-Tip for Photographers

For the best lighting at the Temple of Apollo in Side, arrive 30 minutes before sunset. However, if you want a photo without the crowds, arrive at sunrise (around 6:00 - 6:30 AM). You will have the entire ancient peninsula to yourself, with the sun rising over the Taurus Mountains behind the temple columns.
