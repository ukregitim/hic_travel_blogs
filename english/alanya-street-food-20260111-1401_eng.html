🥙Street Food Guide

The Essential Classics

These are the pillars of Turkish street food, perfected in the warm climate of Alanya.

Simit (The Turkish Bagel)

The quintessential Turkish breakfast on the go. These circular breads are encrusted with toasted sesame seeds and have a distinct crunch from being dipped in fruit molasses (pekmez) before baking.

Best enjoyed: Plain or with a wedge of Kiri cheese.

Where to find: Look for the iconic red glass carts near the Atatürk Monument or the harbor.

In Alanya, the Döner is king. Unlike the processed versions often found abroad, local Döner is made from high-quality layers of lamb, beef, or chicken.

The Pro Tip: Order it as a Dürüm (wrapped in thin lavaş bread) for a portable meal.

Where to find: Efes Büfe near the harbor is a legendary spot that has been serving some of the city’s best street wraps for over 25 years.

Midye Dolma (Stuffed Mussels)

As the sun sets over Cleopatra Beach, the mussel sellers emerge. These are fresh mussels stuffed with aromatic rice, currants, and spices.

How to eat: The vendor will squeeze fresh lemon over the mussel and use the top shell as a spoon. You eat it in one bite.

Caution: Only buy from reputable vendors with high turnover to ensure freshness.

Alanya’S Unique Street Flavors

While you can find kebab anywhere, these dishes have a special place in Alanya’s local food scene.

Gözleme (Hand-Rolled Flatbread)

Watch the Teyze (aunties) roll out paper-thin dough on large circular griddles. These savory pancakes are filled with spinach, cheese, spiced potato, or minced meat.

Where to find: The Tuesday Market (Liman Mahallesi) is famous for its Gözleme flippers.

Historically made with raw meat, the modern street version is a vegan-friendly, spicy mix of bulgur, pomegranate molasses, and over 15 spices.

Serving Style: Wrapped in lettuce leaves or lavaş bread with plenty of fresh mint and lemon.

Buzlu Badem (Iced Almonds)

A refreshing, light snack unique to the Mediterranean coast. Fresh almonds are blanched and served on a large block of ice.

Best enjoyed: In the evening near the Red Tower (Kızıl Kule) as a palate cleanser between savory snacks.

Where To Eat: Markets & Locations

Street food in Alanya is highly localized by the day of the week. To find the freshest and most authentic eats, you must follow the markets.

Day Market Location Highlights

Friday Cuma Pazarı (City Center) The largest market. Excellent for Pide, Lahmacun, and fresh fruits.

Tuesday Mahmutlar / Fish Market Best for Gözleme and freshly grilled local fish like Çipura (sea bream).

Sunday Hacet Pazar Pazarı Great for local dairy, olives, and traditional Alanya-style breakfast snacks.

The Alanya Fish Market (Balık Pazarı)

Located in the city center, this is a hybrid between a market and a street food hub. You can buy fresh fish and have the nearby small kiosks grill it for you on the spot, served simply with arugula and lemon.

Tips For The Professional Foodie

The Ayran Rule: Always pair spicy street food (like Tantuni or Çiğ Köfte) with Ayran, a chilled, salted yogurt drink. It cuts the heat and aids digestion.

Currency: While many shops take cards, street carts and bazaar vendors strictly prefer Turkish Lira (TL).

Timing: Street food peaks twice: once during the market hours (morning to afternoon) and again late at night (10 PM – 2 AM) when locals head out for Kokoreç (grilled lamb intestines) or lentil soup (Mercimek Çorbası).
